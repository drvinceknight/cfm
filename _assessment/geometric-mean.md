# Group-project marking scenarios

The group-project mark combines two scores using a geometric mean:

\[\text{Final mark} = \sqrt{I \times Q}\]

where \(I \in [0, 100]\) is your **individual contribution score**
(engagement: being present *and* contributing to the meeting, as
evidenced by the minutes) and \(Q \in [0, 100]\) is the shared
**quality score** (the group's mark). The full definitions are on the
[Assessment]({{ site.baseurl }}/assessment/#marking-scheme) page; the
three scenarios below show how the formula behaves for three typical
kinds of group.

## Example 1: the free rider

A group of four produces a strong project (\(Q = 74\)). Student D was
recorded as engaged in one of five meetings (\(I = 20\)).

| Student | \(I\) | Final mark |
|:--------|------:|----------:|
| A | 100 | 86.02% |
| B | 80 | 76.94% |
| C | 80 | 76.94% |
| D | 20 | 38.47% |

Student D cannot pass on the strength of their group's work alone.

## Example 2: the conscientious student in a struggling group

Student A was recorded as engaged at all five meetings (\(I = 100\)). The group's
output is weak (\(Q = 42\)).

| Student | \(I\) | Final mark |
|:--------|------:|----------:|
| A | 100 | 64.81% |
| B | 60 | 50.20% |
| C | 60 | 50.20% |
| D | 40 | 40.99% |

## Example 3: a well-functioning group

All four students attend consistently. The project is solid
(\(Q = 68\)).

| Student | \(I\) | Final mark |
|:--------|------:|----------:|
| A | 100 | 82.46% |
| B | 80 | 73.76% |
| C | 80 | 73.76% |
| D | 60 | 63.87% |
