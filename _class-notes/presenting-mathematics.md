## Format

A group-work week. The single hour is **not** a lecture on presenting:
students work through the principles of presentation themselves. This
week is the second of the two exceptions to the question-and-signpost
format, because the hour is given over to watching **student
presentations from past years**.

## In class session

Show recorded presentations from previous cohorts, then take the
discussion that follows.

Past student work is the right material here, and better than a
presentation of your own, for two reasons. It is the actual standard
being asked for rather than a demonstration by someone with twenty
years of practice, so it is attainable. And it is the same 15 minutes,
on the same kind of library, under the same constraints, which makes
every observation transferable.

Show two or three, and pick them at different levels rather than three
strong ones. A presentation that runs out of material at nine minutes,
or that spends eleven minutes on the mathematics and three on the
software, teaches more than a polished one, because the failure is
visible and nameable. Say which band each sits in once the class has
discussed it.

After each one, ask what worked and what did not before giving your own
reading. Questions worth putting to the room:

- How long was spent before the first mention of the library?
- Were the visual aids doing work, or were they a transcript?
- What did you understand about the design that you would not have got
  from reading the README?
- Did every member speak, and could you tell what each had done?

Then signpost the [principles of
presentation](https://vknight.org/pop/) as the thing to work through,
and point at the **Communication** criterion, which covers the
presentation and the paper together and is 30% of the project mark.

A practical note: keep a small set of recordings you have permission to
reuse, and refresh it as cohorts pass. Check before the session that the
group consented to their presentation being shown to later years, and
say in class that these are shown with permission. Students are
noticeably more willing to be in next year's set when they have seen it
done that way.

## What students work through on their own

- [Principles of presentation](https://vknight.org/pop/).
- The [example projects]({{ site.baseurl }}/assessment/example-projects/),
  read with the presentation in mind: each has a paper that a
  presentation would be built from.

## Questions that come up

**"Do we all have to speak?"** Yes. The contribution record and the
presentation should tell the same story about who did what.

**"Is 15 minutes a limit or a target?"** A target. Substantially under
suggests the work is thin; substantially over usually means the design
decisions got squeezed out at the end, which is the most valuable part.

**"What do we do about the live demonstration?"** It is a recording, so
there is no risk of a live failure, but a recorded failure is worse.
Rehearse the demonstration once before recording it.

## Communications

### Before class

Send the following message before class:

    Hi all,

    In this week's session I will show you presentations given by
    students in previous years for this exact piece of coursework.

    You do not need to prepare anything. It is worth coming: these are
    the real thing at the real length, so it is much easier to aim at
    them than at a description of what is wanted.

    Please get in touch if I can assist with anything,
    Vince

### After class

Send the following message after class:

    Hi all,

    Today we watched presentations from previous years' groups and
    discussed what made them work and where they lost time.

    What to work through on your own: the principles of presentation,
    https://vknight.org/pop/

    A reminder that the presentation and the paper are marked together
    under Communication, which is 30% of the project mark.

    Please get in touch if I can assist with anything,
    Vince
