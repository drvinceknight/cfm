From Week 4 a large part of this module is a group project, worth 60% of
your mark. This page is the short version: what you will build, how it
is marked, and what to do this week.

## What you will build

In a group of four you build a Python library that provides tools for a
mathematical problem of your choice. The brief is deliberately broad.
There are eight complete
[worked example projects]({{ site.baseurl }}/assessment/example-projects/)
on this site, written at the level of a *good* submission; read them as
a model rather than as a ceiling.

## What you hand in

Eight files: the paper source, the paper PDF and the bibliography; the
library, its tests and the README; the recording of your presentation
and the record of contributions. Each is described in the
[submission requirements]({{ site.baseurl }}/assessment/#submission).

Every component is graded. You do not need to be the strongest coder in
the group to earn full marks, since the paper, the documentation, the
tests and the minutes are all marked too.

## How it is marked

Your mark is not simply your group's mark. It combines an individual
score with the group's score using a geometric mean, described in full
in the [marking scheme]({{ site.baseurl }}/assessment/#marking-scheme),
with [worked scenarios]({{ site.baseurl }}/assessment/geometric-mean/)
showing how it behaves.

The individual half of that, \(I\), comes from **the minutes your group
hands in**. There is one form for each group-project week, in Weeks 4,
5, 8, 9 and 10, and \(I\) is the fraction of those five at which the
minutes record you as having engaged. The minutes are the only record of
it: a week with no form handed in counts for nobody in the group. The
Week 4 form is completed and handed in during the Week 4 class itself.

## The chapters are yours to work through

There is **no lecture** on the programming topics covered in the
group-project weeks. You work through those chapters of
[Python for Mathematics](https://vknight.org/pfm/) **on your own**:

- Weeks 4 and 5: variables, conditional statements and while loops;
  functions and data structures; object oriented programming; using a
  terminal and an editor.
- Weeks 8, 9 and 10: modularisation of code; documentation of code;
  testing of code.

Yes, this is **self study**, and it is self study **with a social
component**. The reading is solitary; working out what it meant is not.
The first standing item on every meeting agenda is each member
reporting what they covered since the last meeting, which is where you
find out what you misunderstood and where you help someone else do the
same. Reading a chapter, not understanding it, and saying so in the
meeting is exactly what the structure expects of you.

The textbook has tutorials, how-to recipes, exercises and full
solutions for every topic, so you are not working without support. You
are working without a lecture.

## The weekly rhythm

Group work is **on your timetable**: you are not expected to find a free
hour between four diaries. Each timetabled session begins with a short
[meeting]({{ site.baseurl }}/topics/meetings.html) and continues as a
workshop. The secretary writes the minutes, and the group hands them in
at the contact hour.
