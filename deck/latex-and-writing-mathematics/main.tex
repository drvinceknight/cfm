\slidehead{Where is the title?}
\notesref{Writing with LaTeX}{The preamble}

\vspace{0.4cm}

\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
\vspace{0pt}
\codebox{10.8}{\bs documentclass\{article\}\\[0.2em]
\bs title\{A library for ...\}\\
\bs author\{Our group\}\\
\bs date\{\bs today\}\\[0.3em]
\bs begin\{document\}\\
\idt Our library solves ...\\
\bs end\{document\}}
\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
\vspace{0pt}
\ask{This compiles without an error, and the title is nowhere on the
page. Why?}

\answerspace{11.0}{6.0}{%
  \texttt{\bs maketitle} is missing.\\[0.5em]
  The preamble says what the title is. \texttt{\bs maketitle}, just after
  \texttt{\bs begin\{document\}}, is what puts it on the page.}
\end{minipage}

\vspace{0.2cm}

{\small\color{inkgrey} The preamble says what the title is.
\texttt{\bs maketitle} is what puts it on the page.}

\vspace{0.6cm}

\ask{You ran \texttt{pdflatex} once and the paper says \texttt{[?]}.
Which of these four have you not done?}

\vspace{0.1em}

\begin{center}
\answerspace{22}{1.9}{%
  \texttt{bibtex}, then \texttt{pdflatex} twice more. Overleaf runs all
  four for you; a local build does not.}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.4cm}

\ask{And why is `the picture below' wrong, where
\texttt{Figure\textasciitilde\bs ref\{tour\}} is not?}

\begin{center}
\answerspace{22}{1.9}{%
  A figure floats to wherever it fits, which may be the next page. A
  \texttt{\bs ref} is true either way.}
\end{center}

\newpage
\slidehead{What would you call this?}
\notesref{Python for Mathematics, Modularisation}{Break up code into modular components}

\vspace{0.4cm}

\begin{center}
\codebox{20.4}{def process(values):\\
\idt total = 0\\
\idt for value in values:\\
\idt\idt total = total + value\\
\idt mean = total / len(values)\\
\idt squares = [(value - mean) ** 2 for value in values]\\
\idt return mean, (sum(squares) / len(values)) ** 0.5}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\ask{\texttt{process} is not a name. How many functions are hiding in
here, and what are they?}

\vspace{0.2em}

\begin{center}
\answerspace{22}{2.6}{%
  Three. \texttt{get\_mean}, \texttt{get\_population\_variance}, and its
  square root. Each is a line or two, and each says what it is.}
\end{center}

\newpage
\slidehead{Now you want the total with the tip}
\notesref{Python for Mathematics, Modularisation}{Break up code into modular components}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{center}
\codebox{20.4}{def add\_tip\_and\_get\_bill\_share(total, tip\_proportion,
number\_of\_payers):\\
\idt tip\_amount = tip\_proportion * total\\
\idt return (total + tip\_amount) / number\_of\_payers}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.7cm}

\ask{Somewhere else you need the bill with the tip added, but not divided.
What can you reuse?}

\vspace{0.3em}

\begin{center}
\answerspace{22}{4.0}{%
  Nothing. The tip is worked out inside, and there is no way to ask for it
  on its own.\\[0.5em]
  Split it into \texttt{add\_tip} and \texttt{get\_bill\_share} and you
  have both, each usable and testable by itself.}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.2cm}

{\small\color{inkgrey} The `and' in the name is a hint to look, not a
verdict. What it points at here is a useful piece trapped inside.}
